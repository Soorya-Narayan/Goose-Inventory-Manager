# CIP Fundamentals & Safety

## What is CIP?
Clean-in-Place (CIP) is a method of cleaning the interior surfaces of pipes, vessels, process equipment, filters, and associated fittings without disassembly.

## The T.A.C.T. Principle
Effective cleaning relies on four balanced factors:
1.  **Time**: The duration of the cleaning cycle. Longer contact time improves cleaning but reduces production availability.
2.  **Action** (Mechanical): The physical force applied (turbulence). Achieved by maintaining flow velocity (>1.5 m/s) or spray pressure.
3.  **Concentration**: The strength of the chemical solution (e.g., 2.0% Caustic).
4.  **Temperature**: Heat increases the effectiveness of chemicals. (e.g., 80°C for Caustic Wash).

## Safety Guidelines
-   **PPE Required**: Safety goggles, chemical-resistant gloves, and apron must be worn when handling concentrated chemicals.
-   **Chemical Hazards**:
    -   **Caustic Soda (NaOH)**: Corrosive, causes severe burns. Reacts violently with acids.
    -   **Nitric Acid (HNO3)**: Corrosive oxidizer. Causes burns and respiratory irritation.
-   **Emergency Procedure**:
    -   In case of skin contact: Rinse immediately with water for 15 minutes.
    -   In case of spill: Neutralize and contain. Do not wash into drains without neutralization.
    -   **Emergency Stop**: Press the ESD button on the main panel to cut power to pumps immediately.
